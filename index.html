About This Dashboard

This dashboard presents the job market of the North San Joaquin Valley, covering the Stockton and Lodi area, Merced, and Modesto. Each box in the chart represents a single occupation. Box size reflects how many people are employed in that occupation across the region, and box color reflects whichever measure is currently selected above the chart.

How to Use It

  • Box size corresponds to regional employment for that occupation.
  • Box color corresponds to the measure selected at the top: AI Exposure, Hiring Outlook, Median Pay, or Education.
  • Hovering over a box reveals additional detail for that occupation, including employment, typical pay, hiring outlook, typical education required, and the AI exposure score with a brief explanation.
  • The summary bar above the chart updates with whichever measure is selected. It shows a regional summary for that measure, an average for AI Exposure, Hiring Outlook, and Median Pay, or the most common level for Education, breaks employment down into bands or tiers for that measure, and compares it against the other two measures.

Methodology and Data Sources

  • Employment and wages: Sourced directly from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, through its Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics program, using its May 2024 release for the Stockton and Lodi, Merced, and Modesto areas specifically.
  • Hiring outlook and typical education: Sourced from the Bureau of Labor Statistics Employment Projections program, covering 2022 through 2032 at the national level. This program publishes a projection for a defined set of detailed occupations rather than for every individual occupation code, which is standard practice for this data. The occupations with a published projection make up more than half of total regional employment, and each uses its published figure directly. The remaining occupations, which are smaller or more specialized, use the projection for the broader occupational group they belong to, consistent with how the Bureau of Labor Statistics itself organizes this data.
  • AI exposure score: Each occupation received a score from 0 to 10 describing how much artificial intelligence is likely to reshape it, accounting for both direct automation and the indirect effect of artificial intelligence making related tasks faster. A score of 0 or 1 reflects minimal exposure, typically physical or hands on work. A score of 10 reflects maximum exposure, typically routine work carried out on a computer. As a general rule, an occupation that can be done entirely from a home office on a computer, such as writing, coding, analysis, or communication, is considered inherently high in exposure, generally 7 or above. Scores were generated by a large language model and each comes with a brief explanation, visible on hover. These are approximate judgments rather than precise predictions, and a high score does not mean an occupation will disappear.

This dashboard was inspired by the United States job market visualizer created by Andrej Karpathy. His approach has been adapted here for the North San Joaquin Valley region.
